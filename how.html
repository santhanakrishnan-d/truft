Name them

Two Truft bots. Not two companies.

People will meet Truft in more than one place. That is normal. What must not happen is three different memories of the same house.

Bot one · on the website

Ask Truft — for anyone

This is the chatbot on the public site. It knows the projects, the 28 steps, how cost-plus works, and how to hold Friday at Norwest. It can take a name, an email, or a mobile. It cannot open a live job. It will not invent a price per square metre. Follow-up is Truft — a person, or this bot speaking as Truft.

Bot two · after they sign in

Ask Truft — this house only

After someone is building with you, they sign in and get a second bot. Same voice. Different memory. It knows that house: spend, site photos, the pause on the island stone, the waterproofing booking. It cannot see another job. Priya at Henson Way never receives a note that belongs on Mustang Ave.

WhatsApp is not a third bot. It is a door. On a phone it opens the real WhatsApp. On a computer it opens WhatsApp Desktop or WhatsApp Web. Once they are building, that thread is the same house file as the signed-in bot — same pause rules, same facts, same voice. This walkthrough is not wired to a live WhatsApp Business account yet, so the mock will not open someone else’s number. When it is live, the button will open Truft’s WhatsApp.

How you train them

A skill is how you would train a person. Then it runs.

You would not hire a junior and say “be helpful.” You would walk them through a variation: quoted this, actual that, because the finish changed on site, and never invent a date that is not in the programme. You would tell them what not to say on Instagram. You would tell them that unsigned spend waits.

That packet — the dos, the don’ts, the voice, the examples — is a skill. Workweaver turns it into something that can actually do the job: explain variation 12, reply to a public comment, hold Friday at Norwest, draft the weekly letter. You are not learning a new religion called “agents.” You are writing down how Truft already works, once, so the bots can do that work the same way on Tuesday night.

The first two skills that must sound like you are usually these: explain a variation, and reply on Instagram. Everything else waits until those two would make you proud.

Why write it down instead of just chatting with a model?

A chat is a conversation. It forgets. A skill is a job with a name, a pass mark, and a record. When the junior is good, you keep the training. When a letter fails, that failure teaches the next one. When a cheaper, faster writer appears next year, you can move the same skill onto it — if it still passes the bar. Chat does not give you that. Training does.

Quality, not chatter

Raise the bar. Do not buy more words.

Most AI is sold by the pile of words. More drafts, more tokens, a larger invoice. That game is called chasing volume. It is the wrong game for a builder whose name is on a seven-figure house.

Paying for more talk

The machine writes a long weekly letter, a long Instagram reply, a long WhatsApp. You pay for the words. A fluent wrong sentence still goes out. Next month you buy a bigger model so it can talk more. The house does not get clearer. The bill does.

Paying for a pass mark

Before a letter, a comment, or a WhatsApp leaves, it is scored against Truft’s own bar: sounds like you, sticks to facts, does not invent a date, does not spend. Below the mark, it waits on you. A letter at 0.78 is held. At 0.92 it may send. You are not billed for idle software sitting there looking busy.

People who build this call the score an eval — a judgement of the work, not a count of the words. The move is to raise that judgement over time. A held letter teaches the next one. A rumour you mark false stays gone. Overnight the system can pull a handover date that was never in the programme; the bar catches it, so a fluent wrong sentence does not leave the building.

That is also how the writing bill comes down without getting sloppy. New models are almost always smarter, cheaper, and faster than last year’s. Workweaver can move a skill onto a newer writer because the score is the gate. A cheaper writer that fails the bar does not go live. You are not locked to one vendor’s meter. You are locked to Truft’s standard.

Idle software is not billed as work. When a step runs — a letter scored, a WhatsApp answered, a follow-up drafted — those minutes are written on a calendar you can see. That is the timesheet.

This house only

Henson Way is not Mustang Ave.

Search is a pile of documents. Ask a search box about the island stone and it will happily mix last year’s brochure with this week’s pause. A house cannot run on that.

The memory of a Truft house behaves. It remembers that variation 12 was signed on 4 September — brass tapware, quoted $2,180, actual $2,640 because the finish changed on site. It forgets “island stone is included” the moment you mark that false. Tonight’s question ranks above an old brochure. And it will not invent a rate to sound helpful.

A person in the office can see the day. A homeowner cannot see another job. Workweaver is built for that kind of wall. Without it, the product is not fit for a builder.

What “enough context” actually means

Feed Workweaver how Truft already works — cost-plus, the 28 steps, what you will never say, which spend waits, which door is WhatsApp, which door is the office phone — and it can name those rules back to you in a map of the business. “Unsigned spend does not move.” “Never invent a $/m².” “A public Instagram reply may offer Friday; it may not open a live job.” Those lines become the rails every bot runs on. The richer the map, the less you have to repeat yourself, and the cheaper repeated work becomes.

Where this is going

A company that still sounds like Truft when you are on site.

The six months are not “install a chatbot.” They are time in the building: the plan, the first skills, the proof on a real job, the bar set to your voice. That is the immediate need — hours back on site, one enquiry that does not go cold, one unapproved variation that cannot spend.

The longer need is a company that can run more of the day without you in every thread. Not a black box. A junior who gets better.

It finds the rails

Given enough of how Truft actually works, Workweaver can surface the constraints of the business: cost-plus is the open book; extra spend waits; one house per login; Instagram is public comments, not scraping private messages; the office number stays 02 7235 0833. You edit every line. The bots cannot cross them.

Then more of the day runs

Weekly letter. Instagram reply. Follow-up within a minute. WhatsApp that knows this house. You stay on the exceptions: unsigned spend, a letter that failed the bar, a homeowner who asked for a person. That is how a ten-job builder becomes a company that still sounds like Truft at 9:41pm.

Repeated work gets cheaper because it becomes a skill you own, not a rented conversation you pay full price for twice. The tenth weekly letter should not cost the first. The bar stays. The writer can move. The company compounds.

You do not hand over the wheel on day one. Plenty of teams start by planning with Workweaver and running the work themselves. You hand over a job when the record earns it — a letter that keeps clearing the bar, a pause that keeps holding. The brake stays yours.

Who can see a house

Your Amazon. Your key. A ticket if anyone else needs a look.

Names, photos, invoices, and WhatsApp live in Truft’s Amazon account in Sydney. Not on a laptop. Not in Bit Foundry’s company account. Workweaver runs there as software. Truft is the customer — the tenant — of that software.

Bit Foundry can see whether a step failed. They cannot quietly open the kitchen photo. There is no back-channel. Every look is logged.

If something is badly broken and a human at Bit Foundry genuinely needs to see tenant information, it is not a Slack message. You already use Zoho Desk. A ticket is opened. A named person, a written reason, a short window. You can sit on the call. You can refuse; that bug may wait until you do. Amazon keeps a trail of what was opened. That is the unlock. It is not standing access.

If the website sits on Bit Foundry’s Amazon versus Truft’s Amazon

Either way, requests are logged. If the public site is on Bit Foundry’s account during the six months, Truft can revoke that access — take the domain back, shut the door. If the public site sits in Truft’s own Amazon, Bit Foundry does not have a standing key. A supervised unlock is the same as above: a Zoho Desk ticket, a named window, a record. The house file (photos, invoices, WhatsApp) is meant to live in Truft’s account from the start, with a key Truft holds.

What a question to the bot does — and does not do

A question in, an answer out. That pass is not kept to train a public model, and it is not a library of your clients’ houses for someone else to browse. Homeowners never receive a login to the ledger. They see their house. The ledger stays the ledger.

What it costs. What it takes.

This is not a WordPress theme. truft.com.au is a site you likely tuned yourself. Respect it. It cannot be a homeowner login, a pause before money, a memory of the house, or WhatsApp that knows Henson Way.

A custom shop asked to approximate the portals — still without the operating layer — sits on the order of two people, about AUD 5,000 to 10,000 a month. People who can actually build the operating layer well are usually around USD 100 an hour. You are not hiring a standing engineer. You are buying six months of the person in the house, and a way to add the next letter without hiring a shop.

A small share of the writing bill, plus only the minutes a job actually ran. A letter scored, a WhatsApp answered, a follow-up drafted. Not software sitting idle.

The pipes Supplier’s price

Amazon in Sydney, WhatsApp, and the writing bill. You see the supplier page. Nothing is quietly marked up on the way through.

During the six months, and after, you still fund the pipes because they scale with your volume:

  • Amazon Sydney — the machine, storage, and nightly copies. A small always-on box, not thousands of dollars. A quiet month is on the order of a hundred or two. A working month still hundreds, not thousands.
  • WhatsApp — chats a homeowner starts, inside a day, are usually free from Meta. Templates Truft sends first are cents.
  • The office phone — 02 7235 0833 can stay exactly as it is. A programmable follow-up is optional, and only if you want it.
  • The writing bill — at the supplier’s price. A careful writer while the voice is taught. A faster writer later, if the pass mark still clears.
  • The ledger’s machine login — if you want the books to feed the house, Xero’s add-on for that door is about AUD 10 a month.

If you stop at month six — or any time after — BuildPass remains the record for defects, photos, and site communication. Xero remains the record for money. Homeowners are not stranded in a vendor they did not choose. You keep the plan, the cost picture, and whatever proof ran in the house. You are not buying a trap.

What you would set up — and why

A few doors. Yours, not ours.

None of this replaces the site file or the ledger. It reads them. You keep paying for the tools you already trust.

WhatsApp, in the company name

Why: homeowners already write there. A fake chat window on a website is not WhatsApp. You start a WhatsApp Business account as Truft — the name people see is Truft. It can take a few days, and it has to be done in the company’s own Meta Business. It cannot be finished from Coimbatore on your behalf. Keep 02 7235 0833 on the card. The number already living in the ordinary phone app is a different product; moving it is Meta’s process, not a toggle.

The site file stays in BuildPass

Why: photos, defects, the programme, and the handset on site already live there. Copying them into a second system is how facts drift. On a Pro plan you switch on a machine door (Oceania). Published today: projects, photos, defects, meetings, timesheets. If a drawing field is not on that list yet, we use what is published and ask them — we do not invent it.

The ledger stays in Xero

Why: invoices, margin, and contingency already live there. Spend on the house is a view of those lines, not a second set of books. You click Allow on the SS Developers organisation. Unsigned spend cannot move until someone allows it. Homeowners never receive a Xero login. The Xero login lives in your account, in an envelope the operator cannot unwrap.

The website, on a small machine in Sydney

Why: the same system that runs the house can build and host the public site — a small machine in Amazon’s Sydney region, nightly copies, the domain you already own. Not a second agency. Search still needs a clear title and a matching Google Business Profile. We ship a real form today. We do not promise that a stranger’s assistant can book a visit unattended.

Google and Microsoft as the front door to a house

Why: most owners already have one of those. After they are building, that is how they sign in to their job. Apple and Facebook can wait; homeowners must not need Facebook to see their house.
