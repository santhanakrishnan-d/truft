Why Workweaver

Truft sets the standard. The software has to meet it.

  • Truft picks the model. Everyday, careful, or fast. A cheaper model that fails Truft’s checks does not go live.
  • Workweaver connects the tools Truft already uses. BuildPass, Xero, WhatsApp, email, calendar, the website, and the office application. New tools can be added later without starting again.
  • Truft edits the map of how Truft works. Cost-plus, the 28 steps, what not to say, which spend waits for approval. The assistant cannot cross that map.
  • A fix for one customer helps every customer. When a problem is fixed on one job, the fix applies to all jobs.
  • Workweaver stays current. Bit Foundry keeps the product up to date with new models and tools. Truft does not have to chase them.

Quality

Small yes-or-no checks.

A check is a small test with a yes or no answer, written down. Waterproofing is booked Thursday 10:00, or it is not. The $4,200 stone repair is signed, or it is not. The brass taps match variation 12, or they do not. Some people call this an eval.

The assistant has to pass Truft’s checks before a message goes out. If a message fails a check, it waits for a person at Truft. A held message teaches the next one.

Truft’s rules become checks. Never invent a price per square metre. Unsigned spend does not move. Henson Way cannot see Mustang Ave. A public Instagram reply may offer a visit but may not open a live job.

Better checks make a better assistant.

Training

Truft’s process, written down.

A process is Truft’s existing way of working, written down so the assistant can follow it. How to explain a variation: quoted this, actual that, because the finish changed on site. What not to say on Instagram. Which spend waits for approval. Some people call this a skill.

A written process improves the more it is used. A held message teaches the next one. A fact Truft marks false stays false.

Each run of a process is recorded with a time on a calendar Truft can see. That is the record of what the software did and when.

The first two processes that must sound like Truft are usually these: explain a variation, and reply on Instagram. Others follow once those two are right.

Records

BuildPass and Xero stay the records.

BuildPass holds photos, defects, drawings, and the programme. Xero holds invoices, margin, and contingency. The assistant looks them up for one job at a time. It does not copy job facts into a shared pile.

WorkMemory is not the books. It holds how Truft works: voice, checks, and standing rules. Job cost and progress are always read from BuildPass and Xero for that job. If the lookup is not clean, the assistant stops.

truft.com.au is Truft’s choice. Workweaver runs on Bit Foundry.

truft.com.au, the public site, the customer portal, and the office application, can run on Truft’s own machines or cloud, or on Bit Foundry’s account. That is Truft’s decision.

Workweaver itself runs on Bit Foundry’s infrastructure.

The domain stays with Truft. Bit Foundry does not need truft.com.au transferred. Truft names a DNS contact, pastes the records Bit Foundry sends, or points nameservers while the registration stays in Truft’s name.

Indian tax, in plain words

Bit Foundry is a regular Indian company. It is not in a special economic zone. That does not mean Indian tax must be added to an Australian customer’s invoice.

This work is an export of services. The supplier is in India. The customer is in Australia. The work is used outside India. Payment is in Australian dollars. The two companies are not related. For an export like this, Indian goods and services tax on the invoice is usually nil. To invoice at nil, Bit Foundry files a yearly letter of undertaking on the Indian tax portal. That is a written promise to the Indian tax office to follow the export rules.

A special economic zone is a different path. It is not needed for this.

If the letter of undertaking is not filed, Bit Foundry would pay Indian integrated tax itself and claim it back from the Indian tax office. That is Bit Foundry’s cash-flow choice. It is not a charge Truft pays. The invoice is AUD 500, not AUD 500 plus tax.

Bit Foundry does not add Australian goods and services tax. Truft’s accountant may apply a reverse charge on imported services. That is on Truft’s side.

Payment

The invoice

In Australian dollars

From BitFoundry Tech (OPC) Private Limited. To SS Developers Pty Ltd trading as Truft, or the entity Truft names. Amount in Australian dollars. Description of the work. Bank details. Invoice number and due date.

The transfer

International bank transfer

From Truft’s Australian bank to Bit Foundry’s HDFC Bank current account in India. Bank: HDFC Bank Ltd. SWIFT HDFCINBB or HDFCINBBXXX, with the branch confirmed on the invoice. Usually two to five working days. The Australian dollar correspondent bank is typically ANZ.

Account details

On the invoice only

The account number is on the invoice, not on this page. It is a company account, not a personal account. PayPal is not the main path.

Currency. By default, the bank converts Australian dollars to Indian rupees when the money arrives. Bit Foundry may open an HDFC foreign-currency account for export earnings. That account can hold Australian dollars for a short time, with no interest. Amounts received in a calendar month and not used are converted to rupees by the last day of the next month, unless used for allowed purposes.

Proof of receipt. The bank issues electronic proof that foreign money arrived. Bit Foundry keeps it with the invoice.

Reporting. Truft does not write to the Reserve Bank of India for any payment. Bit Foundry’s bank reports each receipt. Every export receipt through the bank is reported. There is no small-amount exception. For invoices up to ten lakh rupees (one million rupees), the bank can close its file on a declaration from Bit Foundry. Software and IT services may also need a monthly export declaration through the bank. Bit Foundry confirms the payment-purpose code with HDFC and its accountant before the first invoice.

Trade number. Some banks ask for an importer-exporter code, a trade number issued by India’s Directorate General of Foreign Trade. A pure service export is often possible on the company’s Indian tax number plus the invoice. HDFC may still ask for the code. Bit Foundry confirms this with the bank. The number is not printed on this page.

The contract will say: invoice in Australian dollars, pay by bank transfer to the company account named on the invoice, due in 14 days unless another date is written.

Disputes

Named by both parties

If both companies name him in the signed contract, he can also decide a commercial dispute. His written decision binds both. One company cannot name him alone.

Courts

Coimbatore, India

Indian law. Courts at Coimbatore for the papers and urgent orders. A written arbitration decision so a money award can be recognised in Australia.

How the liaison and dispute roles work

Under Indian law, a person of any nationality may be an arbitrator. Sakthi does not need to be a company or a lawyer. Both companies must name him in the signed contract.

He must state in the contract that he introduced the parties and that he is also the liaison. If that closeness is hidden, a court can set his decision aside. The two roles are written as two roles.

If a dispute is about him, or either party says he cannot be fair, he steps aside. The parties jointly pick another person within 21 days. If they cannot, Indian law appoints one.

Why Coimbatore courts and also a written arbitration decision

Coimbatore courts remain in the contract for the non-disclosure agreement, the contract, urgent orders, and help with the process.

A Coimbatore court money judgment is slower to collect against an Australian company. A written arbitration decision seated in India is the usual path Australia recognises for a commercial award. The contract does both.
