How the work gets better

Tiny yes-or-no checks.

A check is a small success test. Yes or no. That is the engine. Not more words. Better checks. (If you hear me say “eval”, I mean this.)

You already think this way on site. Waterproofing is booked Thursday 10:00, or it is not. The $4,200 stone repair is signed, or it is not. The brass taps match variation 12, or they do not. Write those checks down. The bot has to pass them before anything leaves.

A pile of words

The machine writes a long note. You pay for the words. A fluent wrong sentence still goes out. Next month you buy a bigger model so it can talk more. The job does not get clearer. The bill does.

A pass mark

Before a note, a comment, or a WhatsApp leaves, it is scored against Truft’s own checks: sounds like you, sticks to facts, does not invent a date, does not spend. Below the mark, it waits on you. A held note teaches the next one.

Turn your rules into checks. “Never invent a price per square metre.” “Unsigned spend does not move.” “Henson Way cannot see Mustang Ave.” “A public Instagram reply may offer a visit. It may not open a live job.” Better checks make a better agent. The written process then improves until it beats that mark.

Idle software is not billed as work. When a step runs, a WhatsApp is answered, or a follow-up is drafted, those minutes are written on a calendar you can see. That is the timesheet.

How you train it

Your process, written down.

You would not hire a junior and say “be helpful.” You would walk them through a variation: quoted this, actual that, because the finish changed on site, and never invent a date that is not in the programme. You would tell them what not to say on Instagram. You would tell them that unsigned spend waits.

That packet is your process. An SOP. Turned into something the bot can run. It improves the more it is used. A held note teaches the next one. A rumour you mark false stays gone. (If you hear me say “skill”, I mean this.)

These run in named time blocks. You see them on a calendar. That is accountability, not a black box.

The first two that must sound like you are usually these: explain a variation, and reply on Instagram. Everything else waits until those two would make you proud.

Why write it down instead of just chatting with a model?

A chat is a conversation. It forgets. A written process is a job with a name, a pass mark, and a record. When the junior is good, you keep the training. When a note fails, that failure teaches the next one. When a cheaper, faster writer appears next year, you can move the same process onto it, if it still passes the checks. Chat does not give you that. Training does.

Why Workweaver

It should get better the more you use it.

You pick the writer. Everyday, careful, or fast. A cheaper writer that fails the checks does not go live. You are not locked to one vendor’s meter. You are locked to Truft’s standard.

Workweaver connects the tools you already have. BuildPass. Xero. WhatsApp. Email. Calendar. The website. The office application. New tools later, without starting again.

The map of how Truft works is the grammar of the business: cost-plus, the 28 steps, what you will never say, which spend waits. Feed that in. Edit every line. The bots cannot cross it. The richer the map, the less you repeat yourself.

When a problem is fixed for one customer, that fix helps every customer. Workweaver stays on the front of the AI wave so you do not have to chase tools yourself.

Henson Way is not Mustang Ave

Job facts stay in your tools.

BuildPass stays the record for photos, defects, drawings, and the programme. Xero stays the record for invoices, margin, and contingency. The bot looks those up for that job. It does not keep one customer’s invoice in a search pile that can mix with another customer.

WorkMemory is not the system of record. It holds how Truft works: voice, checks, process, standing rules. Pointers, not the books. If job cost and progress lived in a mixed search pile, Henson Way could bleed into Mustang Ave. That is not good enough for a builder. So the bot asks your tools, per job, and stops if the lookup is not clean.

Liaison and, if we both say so, the decision-maker

Sakthi Narayanan can sit in the middle.

Sakthi is Australian. He is not a registered company. I still want him in two jobs, if you agree in writing:

  1. Liaison. He helps Bit Foundry and Truft talk. Day to day. This is not a court. Emails, calls, “what did we mean.” Either company can still talk to me direct.
  2. Decision-maker on a dispute. If we both name him in the contract, he can hear a commercial fight and write a decision that binds both companies. That is the arbiter.

Those are two hats. They must stay written as two hats. A liaison who hides that he is also the judge is how a decision gets thrown out.

Is this allowed if he is not a company?

Yes, a person can do this. He does not need to be a company, a lawyer, or Indian. Indian arbitration law says a person of any nationality may be an arbitrator unless we agree otherwise. He does not need a registered ADR firm.

What is not allowed: I name him on my own. After the CORE II / Perkins line of cases, a sole arbitrator picked by only one side does not stand. Both companies must name him in the signed contract.

He must write, in that same paper, that he introduced us and that he is also the liaison. If he is too close to one side and that is hidden, a court can set the decision aside. If a dispute is about him, or either company later says he cannot be fair, he steps aside.

Fallback: we jointly pick another person within 21 days. If we cannot, Indian law appoints one. That is the safety net so the work is not stuck on one name.

Why Coimbatore courts, and also a named decision?

You asked for a contract a court in Coimbatore can hear. That is in the paper: Indian law, courts at Coimbatore for the NDA, the contract, urgent orders, and help with the process.

A Coimbatore court money judgment is slower to collect against an Australian company. India is not on Australia’s short list of countries whose court judgments are registered quickly.

A written arbitration decision seated in India is the usual path Australia recognises for a commercial award (New York Convention). So the contract does both: Coimbatore courts as you asked, and a named person (Sakthi, if we both agree) so a money decision can actually travel. English language. Seat in India.

How it travels

SWIFT from your bank

Your Australian bank sends Australian dollars to HDFC Bank in India. Bank name HDFC Bank Ltd. SWIFT HDFCINBB or HDFCINBBXXX (I confirm the branch code on the invoice). Two to five working days. Correspondent bank is usually ANZ for AUD. Your bank may charge a wire fee.

What I do not do

No public account number

Account number, IFSC, and customer ID go on the invoice and in a mail to the person who pays. Not on this website. Not a personal account. Not PayPal as the main path.

GST, in plain words

I do not add Indian GST on the invoice if this is an export of services and you pay in foreign currency. My accountant files the LUT (Letter of Undertaking) so the export can go out at nil Indian tax. If that filing is not in place, I would have to add Indian IGST. I will not invoice until that is confirmed with my CA.

I do not add Australian GST. I am not registered for GST in Australia. Your accountant may have a reverse-charge on imported services. That is your side. I put a line on the invoice: supply of services from India; recipient to consider Australian GST.

Does the money stay in dollars, or turn into rupees?

If it lands in my ordinary HDFC current account, HDFC converts AUD to INR at their rate that day. That is the default.

HDFC also offers an EEFC account (Exchange Earners’ Foreign Currency). AUD is one of the currencies they list. That account can hold the dollars for a time, with no interest. Unused amounts received in a calendar month have to be converted to rupees by the last day of the next month, unless they are used for allowed purposes. I will confirm with HDFC’s trade desk whether to open that, so a 500 dollar invoice is not forced through a bad conversion the same afternoon.

When the money lands, the bank issues FIRC / e-FIRA. That is the proof the export was paid in foreign currency. I keep it with the invoice.

What you write on the transfer

Beneficiary: BitFoundry Tech (OPC) Private Limited. Invoice number in the reference. Purpose: export of IT / software services. I will put the bank’s purpose code on the invoice after I confirm it with HDFC and my CA (IEC and AD code sit with the bank). If the purpose code is wrong, the credit can sit or bounce. We get that line right once, then reuse it.

You pay the AUD amount on the invoice. Any SWIFT fee your bank takes is yours unless we write otherwise. I will not ask you to pay in rupees.

I will confirm the exact SWIFT, purpose code, and LUT status with HDFC and my chartered accountant before the first invoice goes out. The contract will say: invoice in AUD, pay by SWIFT to the Indian company account named on the invoice, due in 14 days unless we write another date.
