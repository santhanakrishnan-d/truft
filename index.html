How a build often goes, and how it goes with Truft

Here's how each part of the journey often goes, from choosing your builder to getting the keys, and how it goes with Truft.

  1. 01Choosing a builder

    OftenThe kitchen you fall in love with in the photos isn't always the kitchen the allowance (the set amount for fittings) actually covers.

    With TruftYou can see how we keep a job's records before you commit to anything. And please do check our licence on the NSW register before you sign.

    A closer look

    A display home is built to look beautiful in photos, and its price sheet shows a single number. But your block might slope, and it might have a tree you're not allowed to remove, and none of that is in that number. The person who first showed you around is often not the person looking after your build in month eight.

    We build cost-plus, so the price can change, but every change shows up as its own line with the reason beside it. That's also why we'll never price your block from a display home.

  2. 02Your land

    OftenThe first plan treats your block as if it were a simple, flat rectangle.

    With TruftWe walk your block with you first, then give you a one-page summary, before the drawings start to cost real money.

    A closer look

    The slope of the land, any easement, the stormwater, a sewer line under the back lawn, the bushfire rating some Hills streets carry, a protected tree, and whether a truck can get in to deliver the frame will all shape your budget far more than your choice of taps.

    After our walk, you get one page that covers what we could see, what a survey and a soil test still need to confirm, and what council usually asks for with a knockdown on a street like yours. It isn't an approval and it isn't a price. And if you decide to build with someone else, the page is still yours to keep.

  3. 03The price

    OftenA low allowance makes the first price look appealing, and the true cost of the stone you really want only shows up later.

    With TruftAny estimate is clearly labelled as an estimate, and our margin sits on its own line. In the sample home, the taps came in a little above their quote because you chose a different finish, and you saw the new price and the reason before you signed. The stone upgrade hasn't been signed yet, so none of it has been spent.

    A closer look

    Prime cost items are fittings you choose later, such as taps, tiles and appliances. Provisional sums are allowances for work that can't be priced yet, such as rock in the ground, stormwater, or asbestos in the old house. Both usually sit near the back of the contract.

    These are examples, not a quote for your home. Any change you ask for only costs you money once you and we have both signed it. If the ground or a supplier changes a cost, you'll see the new figure along with the reason.

  4. 04The contract

    OftenThe most important clauses only become clear after you've signed.

    With TruftYou get a plain, easy-to-read schedule and your own certifier (the independent inspector who signs off the work). And where the law requires home building compensation cover, you pay nothing until you have the certificate in your hands.

    A closer look

    It's still a proper NSW home building contract, and we'd genuinely love your own lawyer to read it before you sign. Anything written in by hand is the first thing worth showing them. A contract for a new home gives you five clear business days to change your mind after you're given a copy, and published guidance caps the deposit at 10 percent.

    Where home building compensation cover is required, you pay nothing at all, including the deposit, until you have the certificate. Any special condition is clearly marked so it's easy to find.

  5. 05Finishes

    OftenThe price comes through after you've half forgotten what the other options were.

    With TruftYou see a photo, the price and the timing before you confirm. Telling us you love something doesn't lock you in, and nothing is ordered until you've seen the price and said yes.

    A closer look

    Windows often take longer to arrive than stone. If your choice affects a trade who's already booked, you'll see the new date before you confirm. And if a choice changes the price or the plans, it's written up as a variation (a formal change to the contract) that we both sign first.

  6. 06Approvals

    Often"It's with council" can sometimes mean the paperwork hasn't actually been sent yet.

    With TruftYou can always see who has your file right now. Council works to its own timeline, and we'll be honest that we can't speed that up.

    A closer look

    Some Hills knockdowns can go through a faster path called complying development. Others need a development application, which council usually shares with the neighbours, plus a construction certificate before any digging starts. We'll only tell you which path applies once the design has been checked against your block.

    From the outside, waiting on council and a form that was never sent can feel exactly the same, so we show you which one it is.

  7. 07On site

    OftenYou might call three different people and hear three different versions of the same week.

    With TruftEvery photo shows the date it was taken, so if a week ever has no photos, you'll notice the gap straight away.

    A closer look

    You drive past on a Sunday, excited to see whether the frame is up yet. Often the answer to your question comes on Tuesday and doesn't match what you saw from the footpath. Rain is a perfectly good reason for a delay, but you should never be left wondering.

    In the sample home, waterproofing is booked for Thursday at 10:00, rather than "sometime next week". If rain stops a pour, you'll hear what's moving and what isn't on the same day we know.

  8. 08Making a change

    OftenYou mention a change on a Saturday, it isn't signed until three weeks later, and by then the date has already slipped.

    With TruftEvery change is priced and signed by both of us before anything is ordered. A quick chat on site is never treated as a yes.

    A closer look

    Say you'd like stone instead of the laminate in the allowance, and two more power points. In NSW, that only becomes a decision once it's in writing, with the price, and signed by you and your builder. Until then, the work on site carries on as planned, and nothing is spent on that change.

    Your supervisor adds the request to your page before leaving the site, so you see the price and the timing before you sign. We'll be honest, though, that we can't promise the stone will arrive that same week.

  9. 09Paying for each stage

    OftenYou only find out what the bank needs on the day the payment is already late.

    With TruftYou see the stage amount, and what your lender needs, before the stage is finished.

    A closer look

    Many lenders want your own contribution paid before they release their share, and then they send a valuer (someone who checks the work matches what's being claimed). Rather than guess your lender's rules, we ask you or your broker and write them down. Each progress claim, which is our invoice for a finished stage, comes with photos, plus the inspection certificate where that stage has one. The valuer still visits on the day your bank arranges.

  10. 10Quality

    OftenHandover day is when you're left trying to remember every little thing, and the certifier's job was never to check the finishes.

    With TruftWe note anything that needs fixing before it gets covered up, while touch-ups like paint can wait until later. You don't have to be the one keeping track of it all.

    A closer look

    It might be a tile edge that sits a little high, a door that rubs, or a mark on the stone. The statutory warranties are there to protect you: six years for a major defect and two years for everything else, from completion. But they're a slow way to fix something that could simply have been written down while the trade was still on site.

  11. 11Getting the keys

    OftenThree months after moving in, you're asked to explain everything again from the beginning.

    With TruftYour home's records stay with us, you keep your own copy, and your warranties keep running.

    A closer look

    Your contract, signed changes, certificates, manuals and any items still to be finished all stay together on the same page. If someone on our team is on leave, your records are still right there. After handover, we're here to finish any open items and look after your warranties, so you'll never get a sales pitch in their place.

What we can't control

Being honest about the limits

Some things are outside anyone's control. Bad weather can still delay pouring a slab, and if a supplier is genuinely out of stock, it can still take them weeks. Council, the certifier and your bank's valuer all work to their own schedules too. When you send us a message asking for a change, we treat it as a request, and it only becomes a variation once it's written up, priced, and signed by both you and Truft. Until then, the work on site carries on as planned and nothing is spent on that change. Rock in the ground or a supplier's price rise can still change the cost, and if that happens, it shows up as its own line with the invoice and the reason. Whenever we can see it coming, you'll know before any money is spent. We'd always rather tell you about a limit the day we learn of it than promise a pace the build can't keep.

Plain answers

Good questions to ask any builder

So the price can still go up?

Yes, it can. With cost-plus, we can't promise the ground, the finishes and council will match an early estimate. What we do promise is that you'll see every invoice and the reason for every change, that our margin always stays on its own line, and that any change you ask for only costs you money once you and we have both signed a priced variation. If the ground or a supplier changes a cost, you'll see the new figure and the reason, and if we can see it coming, you'll know before anything is spent. If a fixed price would feel more comfortable for you, that's a completely fair choice, and the right time to make it is before you sign. We'd honestly rather miss out on building your home than have you face a surprise in month eight.

What if I take a while to choose a tile?

That's completely fine. Your page simply shows the updated date before you confirm. If a trade has to be rebooked, that does take real time, so we'd rather show you a new date we can actually keep than hang on to one that's already slipped. Taking longer to choose is always okay, and we'll just be upfront about how it affects the timing.

My bank is the slow part. What do you actually do differently?

Before each stage is finished, we show you the stage amount and what your lender told you or your broker they need. We book the certifier for the expected day, and we send a progress claim that already includes the photos, plus the inspection certificate where that stage has one. We can't choose the day the valuer comes, but if your own contribution needs to be paid first, you'll know the amount the week before, rather than hearing it from the bank on the day.

What if we find something wrong after we move in?

Any open items stay on your page, and the statutory warranties keep running: six years for a major defect and two years for other defects, from completion. Just send a message in that same thread. You won't need to explain the whole build again from the slab up, or hunt through your inbox for a certificate.

Sources: NSW Government guidance on contracts for residential building work, which covers variations being in writing and signed by both parties; a cooling-off period of five clear business days to change your mind; and receiving a home building compensation certificate before any payment where that cover is required. Statutory warranties run for six years for a major defect and two years for other defects, from completion. Many construction loans use your own contribution before the loan starts paying, and then send a valuer. How long that takes depends on your lender and on whether all the paperwork is complete. The timeframes shown on this page reflect a common pattern for a Hills knockdown-rebuild. They aren't a measured average, and they aren't a promised date for your home.
